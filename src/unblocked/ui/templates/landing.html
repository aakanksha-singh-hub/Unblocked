The bind

Every reminder costs something. That is the whole problem, and it is why software that stops at the aging report has not helped.

She knows who owes her

Tally, Zoho, any ERP will tell her. The aging report is not the missing piece.

Chasing has a price

Too soft and it's ignored. Too firm and she loses an account worth a fifth of her revenue. There is no free reminder.

So she waits

Waiting is the safest move on any single invoice, and it is what quietly kills the business.

The idea

Non-payment has a cause. Collections software escalates on age — which only works if every buyer is late for the same reason. They aren't.

How one decision gets made

Once per buyer, per day.

1

Read what actually happened

Invoices, payments, and the buyer's own replies — in Hinglish, with implicit dates, disputed amounts and transaction references that have to be checked against the ledger rather than believed.

LLM
2

Work out why they haven't paid

Six causes, as a probability rather than a label. A buyer on a fixed 60-day payment cycle is not the same problem as one who can't afford it, and neither is the one whose invoice never reached the portal.

fitted classifier
3

Score every action against that belief

Twelve actions, from doing nothing to a statutory notice. Relationship damage and the owner's time are charged in the same units as the money at stake, so an action has to be worth more than it costs.

deterministic
4

Guardrails can veto — and usually do

A promise made, a dispute open, someone who said they can't pay, a public holiday, a buyer worth too much to risk. No model runs here. An LLM that can be talked out of a stopping rule is not a stopping rule.

hard-coded
