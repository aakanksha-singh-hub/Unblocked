Method, and what it does not establish

Read this before believing any number in this project.

The problem with our own headline result

This project ships a simulator and an agent. The simulator's effect matrix encodes a thesis. The agent's central mechanism is inferring which buyer is which. The agent then beats baselines that do not make that distinction. That result was determined the moment the effect matrix was written.

It is tempting to defend this by pointing at commit history — the environment was built and frozen before the agent existed. That defence does not work and we do not make it. Commit ordering proves sequence, not independence: same author, same afternoon, and the thesis predates both commits. Freezing a hypothesis before testing it against a world you also wrote is not pre-registration, it is bookkeeping.

Three things that keep it honest anyway

The policy never reads the simulator's parameters Its beliefs live in a separately authored playbook, wrong in two places on purpose — it thinks a soft nudge and a payment link are harmless to a buyer with a grievance; both mildly backfire. Ground truth is unreachable by type The buyer object the agent receives has no cause field. Truth lives in a separate record joined only inside the evaluation. A test walks the agent's entire object graph asserting no truth is reachable from it.

The legal ladder is real

MSMED Act 2006 s.15 caps the payment period at 45 days from acceptance, not from invoice date — so a notice on an invoice accepted late is blocked even when the aging report says 90 days. s.16 sets compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate. Both are gated on Udyam registration: an unregistered supplier issuing an MSMED notice is bluffing, and the agent is not permitted to bluff.

On contact hours we are careful not to overclaim. RBI's recovery-conduct norms bind regulated entities chasing loans, not a manufacturer chasing its own trade receivables — they do not legally apply here. We adopt them anyway, because an agent that reasons "no rule forbids this" about a 10pm message has the wrong disposition to be automating contact with anyone.

Known limitations

  1. Generator circularity — the dominant one, above.
  2. {{ n_priors }} of {{ n_params }} parameters are designer priors, printed at the head of every run.
  3. Promises still carry a small causal effect they should not. The correct treatment is a latent intended pay-date that the promise merely reports. Larger than the remaining time allows; it is the first thing we would do with another week.
  4. The cause model is trained on data from our own baseline policies. Pooling across three reduces the distribution shift against the agent's own behaviour; it does not eliminate it.
  5. Nothing here is fitted to observed data. No public dataset of Indian MSME invoice-level payment behaviour exists, and inventing one and calling it empirical would be worse than inventing one and saying so.
